# Stage 8: the Supreme Court decides

The Supreme Court hears appeals against Special Court verdicts. Convicted people appeal their convictions; the government, on the CIAA's decision, appeals acquittals or seeks a heavier sentence. Below, a "case" or "trial" is one Special Court case, and an "appeal" is one appeal at the Supreme Court. Co-defendants appeal separately, so one trial can have many appeals.

## When convicted people appeal, the Supreme Court clears at least one of them in about two cases in five. When the government appeals an acquittal, it almost never wins

When people convicted by the Special Court appealed, the Supreme Court fully cleared at least one of them in 230 of 567 cases (40.6%). In bribery cases it was about two in three. When the government appealed an acquittal, it won 4 of 420 (1.0%). Most of the convictions overturned were bribery convictions from before April 2021. In 2021 the Supreme Court ruled on money the CIAA had supplied for a bribe in "sting" arrests. Where the appeal judgment cites that ruling, 83.7% of convictions were overturned. Where it does not, 17.3% were.

**Gap type: B, with one A→B input.** The outcomes are the courts' own. The limits on these rates are this research's: the links between each appeal and its trial were built by this research from documents and names (graded, and tested for accuracy), and the court record held covers older trials less well. The one institutional gap that limits them is the incomplete defendant lists for trials registered 2069–2072 (stage 6, A→B).

**Key figures** (unit: Special Court trials registered FY2069/70–2082/83 with at least one decided appeal; firmly linked appeals; final outcomes only; source: court record held on 2026-11-02, appeal links as they stood on 2026-11-03, court case pages fetched 2–3 Nov 2026):

| Who appealed | Against | Trials decided | At least one full reversal | Rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Convicted person | Conviction | 567 | 230 | **40.6%** |
| Government (CIAA) | Acquittal | 420 | 4 | **1.0%** |
| Government (CIAA) | Conviction (seeking more) | 167 | 8 | 4.8% |

- **Where it concentrates** (convicted people's appeals): bribery 66% (192 of 289 trials); fake credential 3% (4 of 156); embezzlement 22% (8 of 37). Amount claimed under Rs 10 lakh: 64% (175 of 274). Verdicts of FY2071/72–2072/73: 20–27%; FY2074/75–2076/77: 52–60%.
- **Robustness:** 38.6–40.9% against 0.5–1.1% across link grades, outcome sources and with mass trials removed.
- **Checked against the judgments:** in 183 of 185 "reversed" trials whose closing order could be read automatically, at least one appellant is acquitted. Of 277 readable judgments on government appeals against acquittals, 276 uphold the acquittal and one partly reverses it.
- **Per person rather than per trial:** 418 of 925 appellants (45.2%). Mass trials dominate this figure; in one forest-felling trial registered in 2069/70, 86 appellants were all cleared.

**What the 2021 ruling has to do with it** (unit: trials with at least one Supreme Court judgment text, 404 of the 567; the counts are a text search for the ruling, 074-WC-0020 of 2078/01/08, and for rule 30 of the CIAA Rules):

| Judgment text | Trials | At least one full reversal | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cites the ruling | 184 | 154 | **83.7%** |
| Does not cite it | 220 | 38 | **17.3%** |

- 154 of the 192 overturned trials with a text (80.2%) cite the ruling. 182 of the 184 citing trials are bribery cases, and 181 were convicted before the ruling.
- Convictions after the ruling were rarely overturned: 5 of 39 trials with a text (13%), against 187 of 365 (51%) before it.
- Per appeal rather than per trial: 186 of 360 reversing judgments (51.7%) cite the ruling, against 40 of 310 (12.9%) other final judgments.

**Why convictions were overturned.** *These readings of court judgments were made with AI software and have not yet been checked by a lawyer. They may contain errors; the judgment itself is the authority.* The ruling's holding, as read: it struck down rule 30 of the CIAA Rules 2059 and held that money the CIAA hands a complainant to pass on as a bribe cannot be used as evidence. 16 sampled full reversals were read with AI software. Main ground: evidence insufficient 8, procedure 7 (6 of them the 2021 ruling), no loss shown 1. In none did the Supreme Court say it had simply weighed the same evidence differently. It placed the failure in the investigation (7), the change in the law (6) or the charge sheet (3). For the 2021 ruling, the Special Court's "error" exists only in light of the later law. A blind second reading of 10 sampled judgments agreed on outcome, stage and trial-court error in 10 of 10, and on main ground in 9 of 10.

**What this cannot establish.**
- That anyone was innocent. A reversal can rest on evidence, procedure or the change in the law.
- Outcomes for people. These are trials; the per-person figure is dominated by mass trials.
- Outcomes for undecided appeals. 2,022 of 4,430 appeals have no final outcome, and they are not a random subset: older and harder cases are decided later.
- That the ruling caused each reversal. A citation is not always the ground of decision. Trials with a judgment text are not a random subset: 192 of 230 overturned trials have one, against 212 of 337 not overturned.
- Anything precise from the sample. 16 full reversals are reported as counts, not as rates. They were read with AI software; no lawyer has reviewed them yet.
- That every link is right. Firm links were 97–99.5% accurate on tests, so a few dozen of 3,304 are expected to be wrong.

## A decided appeal took a median of almost four years

For appeals registered at the Supreme Court in 2008–2018, half of those decided took more than 3.77 years. Counting appeals still undecided, the estimate is 4.10 years. Appeals registered more recently are slower: the estimate is 5.46 years for 2018–2020. Appeals by convicted people take about twice as long as the government's.

**Gap type: A, with B limits.** (A) The delay is the court's own, measured from its own registration and decision dates. (B) The figures are a floor, because undecided appeals are by definition slow ones, and they depend on which appeals are counted.

**Key figures** (unit: Supreme Court appeals; source: court record held on 2026-11-02, appeals of Special Court corruption cases, 4,430 in all):
- Registered 2008–2018: 1,521 appeals, 1,407 decided. Median 3.77 years for decided appeals; 4.10 years counting the undecided; 28.3% of decided appeals took over six years.
- Estimates counting the undecided, by registration years: 3.76 years for 2013–2017; 5.46 years for 2018–2020.
- By who appealed (2008–2018): convicted people 4.23 years (4.66 counting the undecided); government 2.13 years (2.37).
- By year of decision: appeals decided 2015–2019 had waited a median of 2.08 years (676 appeals); those decided 2023–2026, 5.33 years (1,031). This measures how old the appeals being cleared were, not how long an appeal filed today will take.
- 2,128 of the 4,430 appeals had no decision date.

**A selection by case type gives a longer wait.** Counting only appeals whose case type contains the word भ्रष्टाचार (corruption), as the site did before, gives 4.57 years for 2008–2018 (1,064 decided) and 34.5% over six years. That selection leaves out 1,253 appeals filed under fake certificate, bribery and other types, which are decided faster. One judgment moves it: 87 appeals from one forest-felling trial registered in 2069/70, all registered in 2017, were all decided on 2023-06-05.

**What this cannot establish.**
- How long undecided appeals will take. The estimate that counts them assumes they behave like decided appeals of the same age. If the files destroyed in the fire of 9 September 2025 slow them further, it understates.
- Why the wait is what it is (next finding).
- Durations per trial or per person. Every figure is per appeal.

## Three hearing listings in four end without the appeal being taken up

A corruption appeal is put on the Supreme Court's hearing list many times. In 77.1% of listings, the bench did not take it up. Most often the court's own record says the bench did not reach the case, a judge on the bench could not hear it, or no bench sat. Each listing that ends this way typically costs about two months. Of the appeals pending when the court's pages were read, 730 of 1,890 (38.6%) had never been listed at all.

**Gap type: A, with B limits.** (A) The Supreme Court lists appeals that its benches do not take up, and records no reason when a bench "cannot hear" one. Read from the court's own public case pages. (B) The pages cover appeals numbered 070-CR- onward only, and the listing history is taken as published.

**Key figures** (unit: hearing listings and appeals, appeals numbered 070-CR- onward, 3,733 of 3,747 with a page; source: the court's public case pages, fetched 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03):
- **Not taken up:** 21,476 of 27,855 listings (77.1%). Not reached 40.5%; adjourned 18.9%; bench cannot hear 14.3%; no bench sat 3.0%; judgment date moved 0.3%. The court-side reasons together: 58.2%.
- **Adjournments:** 78.9% were requested by a party or its lawyer (the record often does not say which side), 9.0% were for a linked case, 5.9% were requested by the government attorney's office.
- **Decided appeals (1,843):** a median of 8 listings, 6 of them not taken up. 78.1% of all listings before a decision were not taken up. Only 8.6% of appeals were decided without one. Median 8.4 months from registration to first listing, 36.9 months from first listing to decision, 2.0 months between listings.
- **Who appealed:** convicted people's appeals were listed a median of 12 times before a decision, the government's 4 times.
- **Never listed:** 730 of 1,890 pending appeals (38.6%); 466 of them registered more than 12 months before the pages were read. 459 of the 730 carry the court's "file destroyed" flag from the fire of 9 September 2025.

**What this cannot establish.**
- Why a bench "cannot hear" a case. No reason is recorded.
- How many cases each bench was given a day, or how many benches sat.
- Whether an adjournment request was justified. Every adjournment is granted by the court.
- Whether the listing history is complete. Decision dates match the court record for all 1,669 appeals that carry both; earlier rows cannot be checked.
- How corruption appeals compare with other appeals. Only corruption appeals were read.
