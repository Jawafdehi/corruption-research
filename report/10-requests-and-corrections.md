# What would answer the open questions, and what was corrected

## Fourteen right-to-information requests

Each open question in this report could be answered if the institution that holds the record released it. The requests are drafted in Nepali, with the legal basis and a list of the cases concerned where one is needed. They had not been sent at the time of writing, and the law gives an institution 15 days to reply.

| No. | To | What it asks for | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CIAA | The ground recorded for each complaint closed (तामेली) | Why complaints are closed (stages 1–5) |
| 2 | CIAA | Closures later reopened | Whether a closure is final |
| 3 | CIAA | The age of complaints waiting, and time to a decision | How long the backlog waits |
| 4 | CIAA | Time from complaint to charge sheet | How long an investigation takes |
| 5 | CIAA | Its decision on each acquittal with no government appeal found (list of cases attached) | Whether those acquittals were appealed, and why not |
| 6 | CIAA | Its register of appeal decisions, and the number of verdicts it could have appealed each year | A rate for decisions not to appeal |
| 7 | CIAA | The FY2072/73 figures missing from its own report | A gap in the yearly series |
| 8 | CIAA | Whether, and how, a complainant can follow a complaint | Complaint tracking |
| 9 | Special Court | The outcome, sentence and amounts for each defendant | Who was convicted in partly upheld cases |
| 10 | Special Court | Register facts used in the method (low priority) | Checks on the court record |
| 11 | Supreme Court | Every appeal against a Special Court corruption judgment, with its trial number | Linking appeals to trials |
| 12 | Supreme Court | Corruption appeals whose files were destroyed on 9 September 2025, and their status | The arson's effect on appeals |
| 13 | Judgment Execution Directorate | The fine, बिगो, confiscation and prison ledgers for corruption judgments | Recovery (stage 9) |
| 14 | Office of the Attorney General | Acquittals in cases the CIAA did not file | Appeals the CIAA does not decide |

## What the independent check corrected

Before publication, every figure was recomputed from the raw records by code written separately from the code that first produced it: 572 figures in all. 477 matched exactly and 33 differed only by rounding. 57 did not match, and each was corrected. 5 could not be recomputed or depend on a reader's judgement.

Most of the corrections had one cause. The table linking appeals to trials was improved on 3 November 2026, after the first appeal figures were produced. The figures were regenerated on the improved table. The main changes:

| Figure | First published | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Convictions fully reversed on appeal by the convicted | 230 of 565 (40.7%) | 230 of 567 (40.6%) |
| Acquittals reversed on appeal by the government | 4 of 423 (0.9%) | 4 of 420 (1.0%) |
| Acquittals with no government appeal found | 225 of 1,009 (22.3%) | 219 of 1,010 (21.7%) |
| Reversal rate where the judgment cites the 2021 sting ruling / where it does not | 83.8% / 17.9% | 83.7% / 17.3% |
| Appeals that cannot be linked to a trial | 725 of 4,431 | 726 of 4,430 |
| Appeals pending on the court's pages | 1,821 | 1,890 |
| Special Court cases pending registered in the last two years | 75 of 79 | 77 of 79 |
| Names appearing in more than one case | 781 | 779 (the prosecutor's own name had been counted) |
| Complaint screening figures | one year only (FY2081/82) | a 13-year series, FY2069/70–2081/82 |

The full list, with the cause of each mismatch, is kept with the research files.
