# Stage 9: collecting what the court orders

## Nobody publishes how much is recovered from corruption judgments

When a court convicts someone of corruption, it can order a fine, repayment of the amount lost (बिगो) and prison. District courts are meant to collect it. The Supreme Court's annual report publishes one ledger of fines for all district courts every year, but it covers every kind of case together and never separates corruption. "Disposed of" in that ledger also counts prison served instead of payment, and entries written off. The CIAA's reports carry no figure for it either. So nobody can say how much of what courts order in corruption cases is ever paid.

**Gap type: A→B, with evidence.** (A) No institution publishes a fine, बिगो or confiscation ledger for corruption judgments, or splits "disposed of" into cash, prison served and write-offs. We checked all seven Supreme Court annual reports (FY2075/76–2081/82), the CIAA's reports and the Judgment Execution Directorate's website. (B) As a result, this research cannot give a recovery rate for corruption judgments. The only figure for government बिगो recovered, 1.6%, comes from press reports, not from a published table.

**Key figures** (all case types, all 77 district courts; Supreme Court annual reports, Appendix 65(क); unit: rupees):

| FY | Fine ledger | Disposed of | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2077/78 | Rs 18.2 bn | Rs 1.9 bn | 10.5% |
| 2079/80 | Rs 21.7 bn | Rs 2.3 bn | 10.8% |
| 2080/81 | Rs 44.5 bn | Rs 12.4 bn | 27.8% |
| 2081/82 | Rs 46.5 bn | Rs 14.7 bn | 31.6% |

- **The jump after FY2079/80 comes from one or two courts.** Kathmandu District Court, where Special Court fines are sent, accounts for 77% of all disposals in FY2080/81. Kathmandu and Syangja together account for 78% in FY2081/82. Leaving them out, the other courts disposed of 14.6–16.2% a year in FY2077/78–2079/80, and 11.1% and 11.8% in the last two years: the share **fell**.
- **"Disposed of" is not cash.** The one year with a breakdown (FY2075/76, a special campaign) shows 17.5% of fines disposed of as cash. The rest was prison served instead of payment.
- Rs 31.8 bn of fines and about 121,000 years of prison were still outstanding at the end of FY2081/82, across all types of case.
- The CIAA's charge sheets claimed Rs 40.75 bn of बिगो over ten years. No public document says how much a court then ordered or how much was collected.

**What this cannot establish.**
- Any recovery rate for corruption judgments.
- Whether the recent jump is cash, prison served or write-offs.
- A rate for fines imposed in a given year. The ledger is a running stock, with arrears going back decades.
- Anything about confiscated assets.
- The ledger itself does not carry over cleanly: each year's opening balance differs from the previous year's closing balance, by up to Rs 409.5 m. We report this as the source prints it.
