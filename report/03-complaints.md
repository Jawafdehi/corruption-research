# Stages 1–3: complaint, screening and preliminary inquiry

Stages 1 to 3 happen inside the CIAA, which is the only source for them. It publishes yearly counts, not complaint-by-complaint records, so no complaint can be followed from one stage to the next. The CIAA's reports also give screening (stage 2) and preliminary inquiry (stage 3) as one column of outcomes, so the two stages cannot be separated.

## Before any investigation, the CIAA files away between a third and two-thirds of the complaints it closes, and gives no reason for any of them

Each year the CIAA closes thousands of complaints after a first check, before deciding whether any deserve a full investigation. Many are simply filed away with no action (तामेली). In FY2081/82 it closed 29,703 complaints this way, filed away 17,716 of them (59.6%) and sent 927 (3.1%) on to a full investigation. Over thirteen years the share filed away has ranged from 33.6% to 63.5%. There is no steady trend, but the share has risen every year since FY2078/79, and the number filed away in FY2081/82 is the highest in the series. The CIAA's reports never say why any particular complaint was filed away.

**Gap type: A→B.** (A) None of the 13 annual reports (FY2069/70–2081/82) gives a reason, or even a category of reason, for any complaint filed away at this stage. The only ground given is a description of the process: "baseless and unsupported" complaints are filed away. Screening and preliminary inquiry are one column in all 13 tables. All checked in the reports themselves. (B) With yearly totals only, this research cannot say why the share moves, or whether a complaint filed away was later reopened.

**Key figures** (selected years of the 13; unit: complaints disposed of during the year, including those carried in from earlier years; source: the screening and preliminary-inquiry table of each CIAA annual report, 23rd–35th, as held on 2026-11-07):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2069/70 | 6,672 | 3,168 | – | 47.5% | 726 (10.9%) | 2,778 (41.6%) |
| 2072/73 | 16,694 | 10,606 | – | 63.5% | 428 (2.6%) | 5,660 (33.9%) |
| 2076/77 | 16,952 | 9,387 | 786 | 60.0% | 1,143 (6.7%) | 5,636 (33.2%) |

- **Direction.** Over the 13 years the share rose in 8 year-on-year changes and fell in 4. The fitted slope is −0.25 percentage points a year, which is flat. Pooled, it was 55.8% of 82,167 disposals in FY2069/70–2074/75 and 50.9% of 140,396 in FY2075/76–2081/82.
- **Volume.** Complaints filed away rose 5.6 times (3,168 → 17,716) while disposals rose 4.5 times. Complaints sent to full investigation stayed between 306 and 1,244 a year; as a share of disposals they fell from 10.9% to 3.1%.
- **Backlog.** 7,323 of the 37,026 complaints on the books in FY2081/82 (19.8%) were carried into the next year. Their age is not published.
- **Reliability.** Each year's split adds up to that year's printed total of complaints disposed, and on-the-books minus disposed equals carried forward, in all 13 years. Two years have a competing printing with the same total: FY2069/70 (50.7% on an annex's figures, against 47.5%) and FY2071/72 (55.9% on a chart's figures, against 55.8%).

**Why the trend is only suggestive.** "Other action" does not hold the same things every year. In FY2074/75–2077/78 the annexes show that it includes complaints forwarded inside the CIAA and complaints merged into other complaints. Leaving those out, the share filed away in those years would be 65.1%, 57.8%, 73.9% and 78.6%. From FY2078/79 the annexes no longer show internal forwards or merges, and the low of 33.6% falls in that same year.

**What this cannot establish.**
- Why any complaint was filed away, or whether it was later reopened.
- Screening against preliminary inquiry: the two are one column in every year.
- Whether a complaint forwarded between CIAA offices is counted as disposed twice. If it is, the totals are inflated for FY2071/72–2077/78, and possibly later.
- Whether the FY2078/79 dip and the rise since reflect a change in practice or a change in what is counted as "other action".
- Anything for each complaint or official, or for corruption specifically: the tables cover every complaint within the CIAA's jurisdiction.

## On 7 November 2026, a complainant could not track a complaint on the CIAA's website

A member of the public who complains to the CIAA cannot use its website to get a complaint number or check what happened to the complaint. On the day of the check the online complaint page had no form. It carried a notice, dated 18 Kartik 2083 (about 4 November 2026), that the portal is being upgraded, and listed other channels: email, hotline, messaging apps, social media, post or in person. None of those channels comes with a published complaint number or a public status lookup.

**Gap type: A→B.** (A) Checked at the source on 2026-11-07, 20:02–20:04 UTC: the home page, the online complaint page, the notice, the FAQ and the feedback page. None gives a complaint number or a status lookup. The online portal itself was offline. (B) This research cannot see what the portal offered before the upgrade, or whether complaints made by email, phone or in person receive a number privately. Checking would have required submitting a complaint, which was outside this research.

**Key figures.** None. This is a check of what the public website offers, on one date.

**What this cannot establish.**
- That tracking never existed, or that it will not exist after the upgrade. The check covers one day, during an announced upgrade.
- Whether a complainant who goes to the CIAA in person can get a number or ask about progress.
- How many of the 29,703 complainants whose complaints were closed in FY2081/82 were told the outcome. That is not published (right-to-information request RTI-08).
