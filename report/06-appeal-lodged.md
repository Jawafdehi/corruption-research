# Stage 7: whether an appeal is lodged

A Special Court verdict reaches the Supreme Court only if someone appeals. For the state, the CIAA decides whether to appeal an acquittal (or to seek a heavier sentence). The CIAA publishes how many such decisions it takes each year, but not which cases they concern or why.

## About one Special Court acquittal in five has no government appeal anywhere on record

Of the Special Court acquittals that are old enough to judge, about one in five (219 of 1,010, 21.7%) has no government appeal in any record this research could find. These are concentrated in small cases: fake-credential cases, cases with no amount claimed, and cases with one defendant. Not one of the 10 acquittals where the amount claimed was Rs 10 crore or more went unappealed. No public record gives a case number or a reason for any decision not to appeal.

**Gap type: A→B.** (A) No case-level record of a decision not to appeal is published: no case number and no reason. Checked in all seven CIAA annual reports FY2075/76–2081/82. (B) So this research has to infer "not appealed" from the absence of an appeal. That inference is limited by how completely this project's copy of the court record covers Supreme Court cases, and by the CIAA's published list of appeals covering only 2022–2026.

**Key figures** (unit: Special Court trials whose single published verdict is an acquittal, registered FY2069/70–2082/83; source: court record held on 2026-11-02, links between appeals and trials as they stood on 2026-11-03):

|---|---:|

| Too recent to judge | 57 |
| Assessable | 1,010 |

- **Counting only cases the CIAA filed** (leaving out money laundering, other agencies' cases and petitions to annul a decision): 211 of 979 (21.6%).
- **Ceiling with the weak matches:** 227 of 1,010 (22.5%).
- **Where it concentrates** (share of assessable acquittals with no appeal found):
  - by offence: fake credential 75% (42 of 56); every other offence 0–28%;
  - by amount claimed: no amount 37%; under Rs 10 lakh 21%; Rs 10 lakh–1 crore 13% (21 of 159); Rs 1–10 crore 11% (10 of 90); Rs 10 crore or more 0 of 10;
  - by trial size: one defendant 29%; 2–5 defendants 19%; six or more 11%.
- **Amount at stake:** the charge sheets of the 219 claimed Rs 48.9 crore in total (141 of them carry an amount).
- **Cross-check with the CIAA's own count.** For verdicts of FY2075/76–2081/82, this method finds 154 unappealed acquittals. The CIAA's reports record 327 decisions not to appeal in the same years, for every kind of verdict and counted by year of decision. The two are consistent, not identical.

**How an appeal was found.** An acquittal counts as appealed if any of three sources shows it: a government appeal linked to the trial (764); the CIAA's own published list of appeals, 2022–2026 (10 more); or a search of all 33,342 Supreme Court criminal appeal numbers for a government appeal against the trial's defendants, registered within 730 days of the verdict (9 more). Tested on the known cases, that search finds 96% of real appeals (732 of 764), and 99.1% of what it finds is right (737 of 744). Verdicts less than 419 days before the newest Supreme Court registration on record (2026-09-30) are set aside as too recent.

**What eight unappealed acquittals show** (*read with AI software, not yet reviewed by a lawyer*). Eight unappealed acquittals were read in full. In 7 of 8 the reading found no obvious ground for appeal: the acquittal followed the 2021 ruling on CIAA-supplied bribe money, or rested on gaps in the CIAA's own evidence or charge. In 1 of 8 it found a weak ground. A blind second reading of that judgment disagreed.

**What this cannot establish.**
- That no appeal was made. "No appeal found" is not proof: the court record held may lack some Supreme Court cases, the CIAA's list covers only 2022–2026, and the search misses about 4% of real appeals.
- That not appealing was wrong. Not appealing can be correct. Eight judgments are too few to say how often.
- What was proved. Offence and amount come from the charge, not from the verdict.
- Why any decision was taken. No reason is recorded for any decision not to appeal.

## The CIAA declines to appeal in about three of its appeal decisions in ten

Over seven years the CIAA's reports record 802 decisions to appeal and 327 decisions not to: it declined in 29.0% of the decisions it took. Decisions to appeal swing from 20 in one year to 255 in another. Decisions not to appeal stay steadier, between 7 and 76 a year. No reason and no case number is published for any of the 327.

**Gap type: A→B.** (A) No reason and no case number is published for any decision not to appeal, in all seven CIAA annual reports FY2075/76–2081/82, checked in the reports. (B) This research cannot turn the decisions into a rate per appealable verdict. The finding above measures the acquittal side from the court record instead.

**Key figures** (unit: CIAA decisions, by year of decision; source: "decisions of the Commission" table, CIAA annual reports FY2075/76–2081/82): 802 to appeal; 327 not to appeal; 327 of 1,129 decisions (29.0%).

**What this cannot establish.**
- A rate. A decision is not a filing, and the year of the decision is not the year of the verdict. The reports print how many judgments the CIAA received in some years (204 in FY2075/76; 113 in FY2077/78), and split them by verdict in some years only (FY2077/78: 81 of 113 convicted; FY2080/81: 45 in full and 107 in part of 233; FY2081/82: 87 in full and 120 in part of 393). That count is the CIAA's, not the court's, and is not given every year. No rate is built from it.
- Which cases the 327 decisions concern, or why they were taken.
