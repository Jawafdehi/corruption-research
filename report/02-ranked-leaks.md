# Where accountability leaks, ranked

## The ranking rule

Leaks are ordered by two things taken together:

1. **Scale.** How much of the flow of cases, or of time, the leak takes out of accountability.
2. **Evidence.** **M** = measured from a primary record. **S** = measured, but more than one explanation fits. **U** = a question the public record should answer and does not.

The rank says how much weight a reader should give the finding. It does not say what anyone should do about it. "Institution" names who holds the stage and its record.

**Gap type** (the same labels are used throughout the report):
- **A**: an institutional accountability gap. The system does not record, publish, act or explain. Nothing is labelled A until it has been checked at the original source.
- **B**: a limit of this research (our data, tools, time, sample or agent reading). A B is never a failing of the CIAA, the courts or the government.
- **A→B**: both. The institutional gap is the finding, and its effect on this research is stated as a limitation.

## How the findings connect

- **Ranks 1 and 5 sit in the same institution and share one gap.** The CIAA closes most complaints before investigation and, since FY2077/78, most investigations after it. It publishes the counts. It publishes no reason for screening-stage closures, only general grounds for recent post-investigation closures, and no count of closures later reopened.
- **Ranks 2 and 3 describe the appeal stage together.** Convictions are often overturned and acquittals almost never are. About one acquittal in five is not appealed at all.
- **Rank 2's cause lies upstream, at stage 4.** Most overturned convictions in cases with a judgment text cite the 2021 ruling on CIAA-supplied bribe money. In the 16 full reversals read for this research, the Supreme Court placed the failure before the appeal, in the investigation, the charge or the change in the law, and in none did it say it had simply weighed the same evidence differently (*agent reading, not a legal review*).
- **Rank 8 is the other side of rank 2.** Convictions are lost on appeal mostly over how the evidence was gathered. Acquittals at trial mostly fail because the charge was not proved, or the act charged was not the offence. In about one acquittal in six that we read, the court named a flaw in the CIAA's investigation. Either way, the cause usually lies in how the CIAA investigates and charges, not in the trial (*agent reading, not a legal review*).
- **Rank 4 is the time cost of rank 2.** A reversal comes years after the conviction. The listings that end without a hearing are on the court's own record.
- **Ranks 7 and 11 limit every count after the trial.** People cannot be followed through appeal and recovery when the record gives one outcome for a whole case and, for early trials, does not list every defendant.
- **Rank 6 closes the route without a measurement.** Even where a conviction holds, no public figure says what was paid.
