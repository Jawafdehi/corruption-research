# Stages 4–5: investigation and the decision to prosecute

At stage 4 the CIAA investigates a complaint in full. At stage 5 it decides what to do with the result: prosecute at the Special Court, give advice, refer the matter to another body, hold it, or close it without action (तामेली). As at stages 1–3, the CIAA's annual reports are the only source, and they give counts for each year rather than records for each case.

## After a full investigation, the CIAA now closes most cases instead of prosecuting them. The change happened within two years

In FY2076/77, after completing full investigations, the CIAA decided to prosecute 441 times and closed 124 cases without action. Closures were 16.4% of its decisions. In FY2081/82 it decided to prosecute 137 times and closed 794 cases: 75.1% of 1,057 decisions. The change came between two consecutive years, FY2076/77 and FY2077/78 (16.4% → 54.1%), and has not reversed. For the 794 closures of FY2081/82 the CIAA's report gives two general grounds, not a reason for each case. Earlier reports did give one for each case.

**Gap type: A→B.** (A) The CIAA publishes no count of closed cases later reopened. Its FY2081/82 report (35th) gives its 794 post-investigation closures only two group grounds: 754 under one general sentence and 40 illicit-enrichment closures under another. Checked in the report itself. The FY2069/70 and FY2071/72 reports (23rd and 25th) list each closure with the facts found and a reason. (B) Without a reason for each closure in recent years, this research cannot say why the share turned.

**Key figures** (unit: decisions taken after a completed detailed investigation; denominator: the table's own printed total where it prints one, otherwise the sum of its rows; source: CIAA annual reports FY2073/74–2081/82):

| Fiscal year | Decided to prosecute | Closed without action | Closed, share of decisions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2076/77 | 441 | 124 (of 757) | 16.4% |
| 2081/82 | 137 | 794 (of 1,057) | 75.1% |

- The 1,057 decisions of FY2081/82 were taken on 947 completed investigations: 137 to prosecute, 105 advice, 11 referred, 794 closed, 10 held.
- The share of closures was 54.1% in FY2077/78, the year after the peak.
- Prosecutions peaked at 441 in FY2076/77. The CIAA's FY2076/77 report gives the run in one sentence: 144 in FY2072/73, 154 in FY2073/74, 194 in FY2074/75, 351 in FY2075/76 and 441 in FY2076/77.
- Five of the nine years reconcile with the table's own printed total. Five reconcile with prosecution counts the reports print in their text.
- The reports cite section 19(12) of the CIAA Act 2048 and rule 10 of the 2059 Rules as the ground for closure.

**Two documented facts fall in the same window.** The post of chief commissioner was vacant for about five months from Bhadra 2077. It was then filled substantively for the first time since 2073. These are dates that coincide with the turn, not a cause. A real improvement in the screening of complaints before investigation would produce the same pattern.

**What this cannot establish.**
- Why the share turned. The years between the 25th and 35th reports were not checked closure by closure.
- How many closed cases were later reopened. No count is published anywhere.
- How long an investigation takes, or how long a complaint takes to become a charge sheet. The reports print the CIAA's decision date and the filing date for each charge sheet, but not the date of the complaint behind it (right-to-information request RTI-04).

## The Special Court convicts most reliably in cases with one defendant

How a case is charged shapes how it ends. Fake-credential cases almost always have one defendant, and 90% end in a full conviction. Cases of government land wrongly registered average 26.8 defendants, and 12% end in a full conviction. The Special Court gives one verdict for a whole case. A case with one defendant ends in a clear conviction or acquittal. A case with forty defendants almost has to end in a partial result.

**Gap type: B.** This is a measurement, not an institutional gap. That it cannot separate case structure from difficulty of proof is a limit of this research.

**Key figures** (unit: defendant entries and decided cases; source: the Special Court's case record, this project's dataset, court files of 2026-10-08):

| Offence | Defendant entries / decided cases | Per case | Upheld in full |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Government land wrongly registered | 1,098 / 41 | 26.8 | 12% (5 of 41) |
| Illegal benefit | 999 / 135 | 7.4 | 4% |
| Loss to government | 972 / 167 | 5.8 | 19% |
| Bribery | 1,469 / 915 | 1.6 | 47% |
| Fake credential | 642 / 634 | 1.0 | 90% |

**What this cannot establish.** How much of the spread between offences comes from case structure and how much from difficulty of proof. Large cases are not evidence of weak prosecution: trying collective conduct together can be the right choice.

## On illicit enrichment, the CIAA and the courts do the arithmetic differently

In illicit-enrichment cases the CIAA has tested whether lawful income covered spending at every point in time ("cash flow"), while the courts have compared total assets with total income over the whole period checked. The same facts can give opposite answers. Illicit-enrichment charges end in full conviction in 19 of 95 decided cases (20.0%), against 45.0% for all cases, and in outright acquittal in 59 (62.1%).

**Gap type: B.** The disagreement between the CIAA and the courts is the finding. That the effect cannot be counted case by case is a limit of the data this research holds: the court record it uses carries no field for the method or the amount. Whether the courts' own records carry them was not checked at the source, so this is not labelled A.

**Key figures and records.**
- **Outcomes** (unit: decided cases; source: the Special Court's case record, this project's dataset): upheld in full 19 of 95 (20.0%); acquitted 59 of 95 (62.1%); all offences upheld in full 45.0%.
- **The Supreme Court ruling** (decision no. 9433, 2072; [nkp.gov.np/full_detail/8476](https://nkp.gov.np/full_detail/8476)). The case was charged on a cash-flow basis and assessed by the Special Court over the whole check period. Assets over the period were Rs 74,51,426.25 against lawful income of Rs 68,47,535.90. The difference was 8.10% of total assets (Rs 6,03,890.34). The court held that a difference of up to 10% cannot be called unnatural, and the acquittal stood. Ten per cent is this court's reasoning in this case and is not in the Act. *These readings of court judgments were made with AI software and have not yet been checked by a lawyer. They may contain errors; the judgment itself is the authority.*
- **The method is still in use.** Cash-flow reasoning appears in 5 of the 13 CIAA annual reports read (FY2069/70, 2070/71, 2075/76, 2076/77, 2077/78), both in decisions to close cases and in the CIAA's grounds for appealing acquittals, including after the ruling.

**What this cannot establish.** That the method caused the outcomes, or how many charge sheets use it. The record holds the verdict only, not the method or the amount, so this can be read in judgments and reports one at a time but not counted across them.

## Where the overturned convictions come from

Most convictions the Supreme Court overturns at stage 8 trace back to this stage: to how the CIAA gathered evidence in bribery arrests. The measurement, and the AI-software reading of the judgments, are in "Stage 8: the Supreme Court decides".
